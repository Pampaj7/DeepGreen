\textbf{Context.}
Does the choice of language--framework ecosystem carry an energy price in deep
learning? A fair answer needs every ecosystem to run the same experiment,
measured the same way.

\textbf{Objective.}
We quantify how ecosystem choice affects DL training and inference energy, and
what the measurement apparatus must guarantee.

\textbf{Method.}
ResNet-18 and VGG-16 are trained on three datasets at $32\times32$ in
\vEcosystems{} ecosystems (Python with PyTorch, TensorFlow and JAX; C++ with
LibTorch; Java with Deeplearning4j; R with \texttt{torch}; Rust with
\texttt{tch}), \vRepetitions{} runs per configuration. A written
specification, enforced by \vConformanceChecks{} automated checks, fixes the
experiment. Energy is read from hardware counters, with CodeCarbon as a second
instrument. A declared contrast cell of \vSatRuns{} runs repeats both networks
at $224\times224$.

\textbf{Results.}
Training energy differs across ecosystems by \vSpreadTrainMin$\times$ to
\vSpreadTrainMax$\times$ per cell (median between-run CV
\vCVTrainMedian\,\%); among the \vCtrlStacks{} stacks that share one
exported module and backend build the spread is
\vCtrlSpreadMin$\times$--\vCtrlSpreadMax$\times$. At $224\times224$ the spread
within the four LibTorch-lineage stacks compresses to
\vSatLibtorchSpreadTrainVgg$\times$--\vSatLibtorchSpreadTrainResnet$\times$,
while across all \vEcosystems{} it widens to
\vSatSpreadTrainResnet$\times$--\vSatSpreadTrainVgg$\times$, carried by the one
stack without a cuDNN path. CodeCarbon matches the counters on energy within
\vCCmeasDisagreementPct\,\%, but its reported duration overruns the energy
window in \vWindowPaddedPct\,\% of blocks, understating derived power up to
\vPowerUnderstatedWorst$\times$.

\textbf{Conclusions.}
Ecosystem choice materially affects DL energy, by a workload-dependent amount.
The apparatus decides the duration and power fields reported beside energy, not
the energy. Specification, checker and raw records are public.
